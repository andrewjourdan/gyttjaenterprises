Soil Amendment & Topsoil for Growers

Put back the soil your fields lost. Grow in it again.

Every heavy rain carries topsoil off the land and into rivers and lakes, where it settles as rich, fine sediment. Gyttja Enterprises recovers that sediment from scheduled dredging projects and turns it into amendments and topsoil that add organic matter, hold more water and loosen tired ground.

What It Does for Your Soil

Structure first. Yield follows.

Sediment is eroded topsoil coming back home. Worked into your ground, it builds the parts of soil health that fertilizer alone can't supply.

More organic matter

Research blends raised soil organic matter, the base of a living, productive soil.

Better water holding

Fine silts and clays give sandy ground the moisture storage it lacks between rains.

Looser, lighter soil

Lower bulk density means easier root growth and less compaction to fight.

Nutrient holding

Higher cation exchange capacity and calcium help soil hold onto the nutrients you apply.

How to Use It

From delivery to first crop

Sediment works alongside your fertility program. It doesn't replace it. Think of it as rebuilding the foundation, then farming it as usual.

Test your soil

Start with a standard soil test so you know your organic matter, pH and nutrient levels before you add anything.

Pick a rate

Thin rates of 1–2 inches suit most fields. Very sandy ground responds to heavier layers.

Spread & work in

Spread with a manure spreader or loader, then disk or till it into the top few inches.

Plant & adjust

Plant as normal and adjust your fertilizer to the next soil test. Many fields need less over time.

The Research

Decades of field and greenhouse results

Universities and state programs have grown crops in dredged sediment for decades. Here's what they found.

Ohio demonstration farm

Researchers spread Lake Erie harbor sediment on working fields and reported improved soil health and higher crop yields. The added material also offset compaction from heavy equipment.

Ohio Sea Grant, 2026 →

Soil health in greenhouse trials

Blending dredged sediment into farm soil raised organic matter, cation exchange capacity and calcium and lowered bulk density. Soybean biomass and yields rose with higher sediment ratios.

Brigham et al., BGSU, 2021 →

Four years on sandy soil

University of Illinois plots topped Plainfield sand with Illinois River sediment. Corn showed higher growth and grain yields, and soybeans grew more vigorously too.

Darmody & Diaz, 2017 →

Vegetables in the open field

Lettuce, radish and tomato trials with Lake Erie sediment showed positive crop responses in an open-field production system.

Sequeira et al., 2024 →

Topsoil-grade properties

Peoria Lakes sediment is mostly silt and clay with about 2–6% organic matter and a pH of 7 or higher, comparable to quality Illinois topsoil.

Marlin & Darmody, ISTC TR-068, 2018 →

Mud to Parks

About 105,000 tons of Peoria Lake sediment went by barge to a bare Chicago slag field. It weathered into loose soil that now grows grass, prairie and trees.

Illinois Sustainable Technology Center →

Questions

Common questions from growers

Is this the same as topsoil?

Close to it. Most river and lake sediment is topsoil that eroded off the land. Once it's dewatered and weathered, it handles and grows much like native topsoil. Our Screened Topsoil is finished for that use.

Does it replace fertilizer?

No. It improves organic matter, structure and water holding, and it brings some nutrients with it. You'll still need a fertility program, but many growers can fine-tune rates down after follow-up soil tests.

How much should I apply?

Most fields do well with 1–2 inches worked into the topsoil. Very sandy ground can take heavier layers. Start with a soil test and a trial strip, then scale up.

Is it certified organic?

Not yet. Gyttja Enterprises is in development, and certification would come with product launch. Ask us about your certifier's requirements.

How is it delivered?

Bulk by truck, or by barge to river terminals where available, which keeps freight costs down. The Compost Blend will also be offered bagged.

Is it tested?

Yes. Every lot is lab-tested, and reports are available on request.
